A front-row seat to a global revolution

In the late 1990s, Dublin was becoming Europe’s Silicon Valley. Three professionals from Brazil, Peru, and Portugal ended up right in the middle of it.

Ireland’s capital had emerged as a burgeoning hub for technology and innovation, and the localization industry was changing fast alongside it. Amid that dynamic environment, three newcomers found themselves at the forefront of a profession that was still figuring out what it wanted to be.

Three Amigos and a Global Revolution chronicles the evolution of that industry over three transformative decades. It starts with the manual processes of the floppy disk era and runs all the way to the arrival of AI-powered, real-time content creation. Along the way, it offers a comprehensive look at the technological advances and the collaborative efforts that redefined globalization.

The book combines industry insight with a historical perspective. It is a story about tools and systems, sure, but mostly it is about the people behind them, and the resilience, innovation, and strategic collaboration that carried the industry from one era to the next.

It closes by looking ahead, to a methodology called Intent-Driven Content Creation (IDCC), in which AI generates market-appropriate content from semantic intent instead of translating from a source document. If translation was the first chapter of this story, that is where the authors think the next one begins.

What the book covers

  1. The floppy disk era

    Localization as a hands-on craft. Files moved by hand, teams were spread across countries, and getting a product out in another language took a lot of patience and a lot of coordination.

  2. Memory and management

    Translation memory and translation management systems turn one-off projects into repeatable, scalable pipelines.

  3. Machines join the conversation

    Machine translation arrives, then neural machine translation changes what quality means and what people expect from it.

  4. An ecosystem takes shape

    Language service providers, content management system integrations, and the push for simultaneous multilingual release reshape how global products are built and shipped.

  5. Beyond translation

    Transcreation and content orchestration move the conversation from converting words to creating the right experience for each market.

  6. Intent-Driven Content Creation

    IDCC: AI generates market-appropriate content from semantic intent, with no source document to translate from.

Three engineers, three countries, one Dublin office

They met at Microsoft’s localization operation in Dublin in the late 1990s, and they have been sharing notes on the industry ever since.

Agustín Da Fieno Delucchi

From Lima, Peru

Agustín’s background is in computer science. After graduating in 1993, he spent several years working as an independent software developer in Lima, building software for companies, government agencies, and educational institutions.

He moved to Dublin in 1998 to join Microsoft as a Language Specialist. Since then he has held a wide range of roles, both as an individual contributor and as a manager, across engineering, program management, compliance, architecture, data science, and AI.

Today his work centers on using artificial intelligence and data science to build technology that is globally accessible, efficient, and centered on people. He is a strong advocate for ethical AI design and governance, and for technologies that augment human potential rather than replace it. He is also an instructor for the Certificate in Localization at the University of Washington.

Alfredo de Almeida

From Rio de Janeiro, Brazil

Alfredo holds a degree in Computer Science, with a focus on software engineering, from the Universidade Federal do Rio de Janeiro. He began his localization career in his hometown, working as a software engineer and project manager at IDOC, a local localization supplier.

In April 1995, he moved to Dublin to join Microsoft as a Brazilian Portuguese Localizer. In 2000, he relocated to Seattle, where he continued a Microsoft career that would span 30 years, serving in a range of roles as both an individual contributor and a manager. His path took him through Engineering, Program Management, Localization Architecture, Data Science, and Artificial Intelligence until his retirement in 2025.

His expertise spans Agile software engineering, business analytics, international project management, internationalization and globalization, user experience, and linguistics. Since 2012, he has also served as an instructor for the Certificate in Localization at the University of Washington.

Jorge Russo dos Santos

From Santo Tirso, Portugal

Jorge graduated as an electronic engineer in 1999 from the Universidade do Minho in Guimarães. Right after college he moved to Dublin, where he joined Microsoft as a Software Localization Engineer.

He left Microsoft in 2015 and has kept working in localization at some of the biggest names in tech, including Cisco, Workday, Meta, Amazon, Block, and Salesforce. He is a PMP-certified program manager with a track record of delivering projects on time and on budget, and plenty of experience leading international teams across locations and time zones.

His skills cover localization, localizability, internationalization, and globalization, in both waterfall and agile environments. Like his co-authors, he is an instructor for the Certificate in Localization at the University of Washington.

Between them, the three authors have worked at Microsoft, Cisco, Workday, Meta, Amazon, Block, and Salesforce, and all three teach the next generation of localization professionals. This book is what happens when you put twenty-plus years of that experience in one place.
